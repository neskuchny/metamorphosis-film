# AI risk rubric

Adapted from the metAImorphosis toolbox (A. Tarasov, M. Belukhin, 2026, CC BY). Use it to set `risk.level` and the tone of the film.

## Three questions

1. Is the market big and growing?
2. Does the business have healthy margins and meaningful size?
3. Can its competitive advantage be repeated quickly by someone with enough capital?

Three "yes" → AI-native and AI-enabled competitors will come after it → **high**.
Two "yes" → **medium**. One or none, or a strong protective factor below → **low** (the film still gets made — about erosion at the edges and the opportunity).

## Mid-sized industries with higher AI risk

Trade (retail and wholesale) · Construction · Professional, scientific and technical services · Administrative and support services · Manufacturing · Transportation and storage · Real estate · Financial services.

Two typical risk shapes:
- **Businesses that depend on individuals** (business development, expertise, licences): the best people are the first target of AI-native competitors — better tools, better pay, comfortable conditions.
- **Mass-market businesses**: in 3–4 years most moats disappear; AI-native companies are several times more efficient in marketing, distribution and internal operations.

## Protective factors (lower risk)

Luxury · Critical safety impact · High-complexity legacy physical systems · Unique operating licence · Individual human liability · Long-term exclusive public concessions · Non-reproducible physical assets.

A protective factor does not make the risk zero: the challenger still attacks the edges — sales, service, back office, hiring.

## The biggest internal risk

Inertia and the comfort zone of the existing team. They will produce every argument against change. The film should anticipate those arguments ("we have relationships", "our clients are conservative", "it doesn't work yet", "we'll adopt it later") and answer them with evidence, not pressure.

## How AI-native companies operate (use as the challenger's traits)

- Business and data are described and structured so AI can run them.
- Input data turns into actions with little human-intensive process.
- Very high speed of decisions and operations.
- Experienced, autonomous individual contributors, each managing several AI agents; minimal middle management.
- Continuous rapid experimentation in product and internal processes; learnings scale to the whole organisation in minutes.
